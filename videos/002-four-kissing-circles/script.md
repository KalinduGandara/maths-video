# Four Touching Circles

**Concept:** Three circles of radius 1, 2 and 3 touch each other and a small circle of radius r. Joining the centres gives a 3-4-5 right triangle, so we can use coordinates. The three distance equations are expanded and subtracted in pairs; every simplification step is shown. This gives x = 1 − r/3 and y = 1 − r/2. Substituting back and simplifying gives 23r² + 132r − 36 = 0, and the quadratic formula gives r = 6/23 (answer B). ## Full narration

Three circles, with radii one, two and three, all touch each other. A smaller circle sits in the gap between them. Let's find its radius.

Here is the question. A circle of radius r is surrounded by three circles with radii one, two and three. Each of them touches the small circle on the outside, and they also touch each other. What is r? The options are one quarter, six over twenty three, three over eleven, five over seventeen, and three over ten.

Working with the curves directly is hard. For touching circles, it is much easier to work with the centres. So let's mark the centre of each circle.

When two circles touch on the outside, the line joining their centres passes through the point where they touch. So the distance between the centres equals the sum of the two radii.

Call the centres A, B and C. Then A B is one plus two, which is three. A C is one plus three, which is four. And B C is two plus three, which is five.

The sides are three, four and five. Three squared plus four squared is nine plus sixteen, which is twenty five, and that is five squared. So by the converse of Pythagoras, the angle at A is a right angle.

Because of the right angle, we can place the triangle on coordinate axes. Put point A at the origin, B at three, zero on the x axis, and C at zero, four on the y axis.

Now the small circle. Let its centre be O, at the point x, y. It touches all three circles, so by the same rule, O A is r plus one, O B is r plus two, and O C is r plus three.

Using the distance formula, each of these gives an equation. x squared plus y squared equals one plus r, all squared. x minus three, squared, plus y squared equals two plus r, squared. And x squared plus y minus four, squared, equals three plus r, squared.

That's three equations with three unknowns. When we expand the brackets, each equation contains x squared, y squared and r squared. So if we subtract one equation from another, those squared terms cancel.

Start with equation two. x minus three, squared, expands to x squared minus six x plus nine. And two plus r, squared, expands to four plus four r plus r squared.

Equation one expands the same way. One plus r, squared, is one plus two r plus r squared.

Now subtract equation one from equation two. The x squared, y squared and r squared terms cancel, leaving minus six x plus nine equals three plus two r.

Subtract nine from both sides, so minus six x equals minus six plus two r. Then divide both sides by minus six. That gives x equals one minus r over three.

Now equation three. y minus four, squared, is y squared minus eight y plus sixteen. And three plus r, squared, is nine plus six r plus r squared.

Subtract the expanded equation one again. The squared terms cancel, and we get minus eight y plus sixteen equals eight plus four r.

Subtract sixteen from both sides, so minus eight y equals minus eight plus four r. Divide by minus eight, and y equals one minus r over two.

Now x and y are both written in terms of r. Substitute them into equation one. This gives one minus r over three, squared, plus one minus r over two, squared, equals one plus r, squared.

Expand each bracket. One minus r over three, squared, is one minus two r over three plus r squared over nine. One minus r over two, squared, is one minus r plus r squared over four. And one plus r, squared, is one plus two r plus r squared.

To clear the fractions, multiply every term by thirty six, since both nine and four divide into it. Thirty six times two r over three is twenty four r. Thirty six times r squared over nine is four r squared, and thirty six times r squared over four is nine r squared.

Now collect like terms on the left. Thirty six plus thirty six is seventy two. Minus twenty four r minus thirty six r is minus sixty r. And four r squared plus nine r squared is thirteen r squared.

Move everything to the right hand side. Thirty six minus thirteen is twenty three, seventy two plus sixty is one hundred and thirty two, and thirty six minus seventy two is minus thirty six. So twenty three r squared plus one hundred and thirty two r minus thirty six equals zero.

This does not factor easily by sight, so use the quadratic formula, with a equals twenty three, b equals one hundred and thirty two, and c equals minus thirty six.

First, the part under the square root. One hundred and thirty two squared is seventeen thousand, four hundred and twenty four. Minus four times twenty three times minus thirty six is plus three thousand, three hundred and twelve. Together that's twenty thousand, seven hundred and thirty six, which is one hundred and forty four squared.

So r equals minus one hundred and thirty two, plus or minus one hundred and forty four, all over forty six. With the plus sign, r is twelve over forty six, which simplifies to six over twenty three. With the minus sign, r is minus two hundred and seventy six over forty six, which is minus six.

A radius can't be negative, so r is six over twenty three. The answer is B.

As a quick check, six over twenty three is about zero point two six. That's roughly a quarter of the radius of the smallest circle, which matches the picture.

There is also a formula called Descartes' circle theorem, which links the radii of four touching circles. It gives the same answer in one line. If you'd like a video on how that theorem works, let me know in the comments.
