# The Bitten Semicircle

**Concept:** A semicircle on diameter AB has a chord CD of length 16 parallel to AB. A smaller semicircle on AB, tangent to CD, is cut out. The shaded area is ½π(R² − r²). Tangency makes the height of CD equal to r, so the right triangle (centre, tangent point, D) gives R² − r² = 8² = 64. Area = 32π (answer C). A live "shrink the bite" check shows the area stays 32π for any r, as long as the chord is 16.
**Audience:** high school / competition prep · **Duration:** 3:10 · **Theme:** Neon Pulse

## Full narration

A semicircle with a bite taken out of it. We only know one number, sixteen. Can that really be enough to find the shaded area?

Here is the setup. A big semicircle sits on its diameter, A B. A chord, C D, of length sixteen, runs parallel to A B. A smaller semicircle, also standing on A B, just touches C D, and it is cut away.

The question asks for the area of the shaded shape. The choices are sixteen pi, twenty four pi, thirty two pi, forty eight pi, and sixty four pi.

We do not know either radius, so let's give them names. Call the big radius capital R, and the small radius little r.

The shaded area is the big half disc minus the small half disc. That is one half pi R squared, minus one half pi r squared.

Both terms share one half pi, so pull it out. The area is one half pi, times R squared minus r squared. So we only need that difference, not each radius on its own.

Now use the tangent point. The small semicircle touches C D at its very top, directly above the centre. So the distance from A B up to C D is exactly little r.

Next, draw the big radius from the centre to D. Its length is capital R. And because the centre sits right below the middle of C D, the point M splits the chord into two halves of eight.

That gives a right triangle. By Pythagoras, r squared plus eight squared equals R squared. Rearranging, R squared minus r squared equals sixty four.

And that is the key idea. We never needed the radii themselves, only the difference of their squares, and the chord hands it to us directly.

Substitute it into the area. One half pi, times sixty four, equals thirty two pi.

So the shaded area is thirty two pi. That's answer C.

Here is a fun check. Keep the chord at sixteen and shrink the small semicircle. The radii change, but the area never does. When the bite vanishes, C D becomes the diameter, R is eight, and the area is still thirty two pi.

To recap. Write the area as one half pi, times R squared minus r squared. Use the tangent point to build a right triangle with half of the chord. Pythagoras gives the difference, sixty four, so the area is thirty two pi.

Next time two radii show up together, look for the right triangle that links them.
